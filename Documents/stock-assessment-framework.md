# Stock Assessment Framework

A three-gate process for evaluating individual stock candidates: fundamentals → valuation-vs-expectations → technicals. Each gate answers a different question. A candidate should clear each gate before moving to the next — no point timing an entry into a business that fails the fundamentals check.

---

## Gate 1: Fundamentals — "Is this a good business?"

Answers: business quality, independent of price.

- [ ] **Revenue growth** — is it above a threshold you've set (e.g. double-digit YoY), and is the trend accelerating, stable, or decelerating?
- [ ] **Margin trend** — gross/operating/EBITDA margins stable or improving, not eroding?
- [ ] **Free cash flow** — growing, and is it funding the growth (vs. debt-funded)?
- [ ] **Balance sheet** — leverage reasonable for the sector; no near-term refinancing red flags?
- [ ] **Competitive position** — durable moat, customer concentration risk, or exposure to a single supplier/customer that could disrupt the thesis?
- [ ] **Guidance quality** — has management been beating, meeting, or missing their own guidance recently?

**Gate result:** Pass / Fail. If Fail, stop — don't proceed to Gate 2.

---

## Gate 2: Valuation vs. Expectations — "Is the good news already priced in?"

Answers: whether the market has already caught up to the story.

- [ ] **Valuation multiple** — P/E, EV/EBITDA, PEG vs. sector peers and vs. the stock's own 3–5 year history. Rich, fair, or cheap?
- [ ] **Analyst target-to-price gap** — not just the rating label. A "Buy" with a price target *below* current price is a different signal than a "Buy" with 20%+ implied upside. Check the gap, not just the tag.
- [ ] **Target trend** — have targets been raised recently (catching up to good news) or are they lagging a recent rally (signal the rally may be ahead of fundamentals)?
- [ ] **Recent price action context** — is the stock near an all-time high / just off one? Rapid re-ratings deserve extra scrutiny here.

**Gate result:** Pass / Caution / Fail.
- Caution doesn't mean stop — it means proceed to Gate 3 with a bias toward waiting for a better entry rather than buying at market.

---

## Gate 3: Technicals — "Is now a good time?"

Answers: trend and timing, not whether to own it at all.

**Trend check:**
- [ ] Price above both 50-day and 200-day moving averages?
- [ ] 50-day above 200-day (golden cross) rather than below (death cross)?
- [ ] Is the uptrend confirmed across multiple sources, or do they disagree? (See consistency check below.)

**Stretch check:**
- [ ] RSI (14-day) — above 70 (overbought) or below 30 (oversold)?
- [ ] Price position within Bollinger Bands — pinned near the upper band = stretched short-term, even in a genuine uptrend.
- [ ] Recent break of a support or resistance level — which direction, and how recently?

**Consistency check (do this before trusting any single read):**
- [ ] Pull 2–3 sources for the same indicators. If they materially disagree (common — depends heavily on the exact date pulled), treat it as "no clear signal" rather than picking whichever is more convenient.

**Gate result → Action, not just a verdict:**
- Trend confirmed + not stretched → consider entry near market
- Trend confirmed + stretched → wait for a pullback to the 50-day MA or a defined support level; don't chase
- Trend not confirmed / mixed signals → no clear technical case either way; wait for confirmation

---

## Before Acting: Position Sizing & Invalidation

- [ ] **Bucket** — is this core or satellite/speculative? Decide before researching, not after.
- [ ] **Size** — reflect the uncertainty in the process itself (data staleness, source disagreement, priced-in risk), not just conviction in the thesis.
- [ ] **Invalidation point** — define in advance what would prove the thesis wrong: a support break, a guidance cut, a specific fundamental deterioration. Write it down before entry, not after.

---

## Quick Reference: What Tripped Up the AVGO / GE / LLY Exercise

- Fundamentals-only research (sell-side "best of" articles) skipped Gates 2 and 3 entirely — led to picks that all had unconfirmed or stretched technicals underneath a good business story.
- AYA's "Buy" rating had an average price target *below* the current price — a Gate 2 failure that a rating label alone wouldn't catch.
- LLY's technical read flatly contradicted itself depending on source and date — a reminder to always run the consistency check before trusting a single "Strong Buy/Sell" tag.
